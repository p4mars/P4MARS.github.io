Swarm Localization for Planetary Rovers

No longer available

master-project
Working on it Philipp Kunze

A swarm of small planetary rovers can explore far more of the Moon or Mars than a single large rover, and can keep working when one member fails. On another planet there is no GNSS, no prepared map and no magnetic north, so every rover must work out where it is relative to the others from what it can sense itself. This project studies relative localization within such a rover swarm.

What you will work on

  • Fusing inter-rover measurements, such as range, bearing or visual detections of neighbours, with onboard odometry into a consistent estimate of the swarm geometry.
  • Handling the conditions of planetary terrain: wheel slip on loose regolith, long shadows and harsh lighting, few distinctive landmarks, and rovers that drop out of line of sight behind rocks and crater rims.
  • Keeping the estimate consistent as rovers join, leave or lose contact, with measurements arriving asynchronously over a limited communication link.
  • Evaluating accuracy, robustness and scaling behaviour in simulation and on small ground robots in the lab.

What we look for

  • An MSc student in robotics, aerospace, computer science or a related field.
  • Background in state estimation, such as Kalman or factor-graph methods, and solid Python or C++.
  • Interest in planetary exploration and in methods that must work without external infrastructure.
